ZORVEX TECHNOLOGIES — INCIDENT MANAGEMENT AND ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS STANDARD
Policy ID ENG-RCA-005 | Version 2.0 | Effective 1 November 2026
Owner: Engineering Operations

1. PURPOSE
Defines detection, severity, response, communication, recovery, post-incident review, and corrective action tracking. Fictional technical training content.

2. DEFINITIONS
Incident: unplanned interruption or degradation.
Major incident: significant customer, security, financial, regulatory, or operational impact.
Root cause: underlying condition that made the incident possible.
Contributing factor: condition increasing likelihood, duration, or impact.
Mitigation: action reducing impact without necessarily removing root cause.

3. SEVERITY
SEV-1 Critical: broad outage, major data integrity issue, active compromise, or severe business impact. Immediate incident command and executive notification.
SEV-2 High: significant degradation of important service or customer group, limited workaround.
SEV-3 Medium: limited impact or viable workaround.
SEV-4 Low: minor defect or informational event.
Incident commander may change severity and must record rationale.

4. DETECTION AND DECLARATION
Sources include monitoring, customer reports, service desk, security tools, and employee observations. Record time, service, symptoms, and evidence. Notify on-call engineer and incident commander immediately for suspected SEV-1/SEV-2.

5. ROLES
Incident Commander coordinates priorities and response.
Technical Lead directs diagnosis and remediation.
Communications Lead sends approved updates.
Scribe records timeline and decisions.
Service Owner validates recovery and follow-up.
Responsibilities must be explicit, even when one person holds multiple roles.

6. RESPONSE WORKFLOW
Acknowledge alert; declare severity; open incident channel and record; assess scope and impact; stabilize; choose mitigation; communicate; verify recovery; monitor; close operational incident; schedule review. Preserve evidence without exposing secrets or unnecessary personal data.

7. COMMUNICATION
For SEV-1, internal updates at least every 30 minutes; SEV-2 at least hourly while impact continues. State known impact, actions, workaround, and next update time. Do not speculate or assign blame. Customer communications follow the approved plan.

8. RCA REQUIRED SECTIONS
Incident ID/title; date; severity; service owner/authors; executive summary; customer impact; start/detection/mitigation/resolution times; timeline; technical root cause; contributing factors; detection gaps; what went well; what failed; remediation; lessons learned; action owners and due dates. Separate confirmed facts from hypotheses.

9. BLAMELESS REVIEW
Focus on system conditions, design, safeguards, monitoring, documentation, and process. Do not attribute failure to personal character or intent. Misconduct and security investigations follow separate confidential processes.

10. CORRECTIVE ACTIONS
Every action needs description, priority, accountable owner, due date, tracking ticket, and measurable completion criteria. Valid actions include tests, code fixes, capacity changes, alerts, runbook updates, access changes, training, or architecture work. "Be more careful" is not an acceptable action.

11. REVIEW DEADLINES
Review SEV-1/SEV-2 incidents normally within 5 working days and useful SEV-3 reviews within 10 working days. Publish approved RCA after security/privacy review. Track overdue actions in engineering reviews.

12. SECURITY AND DATA INCIDENTS
Report suspected exposure, credential compromise, malware, or unauthorized access immediately through Security process. Do not delete logs, independently notify external parties, or investigate beyond authority. Legal/privacy teams decide regulatory notifications.

13. EXAMPLE
A reporting API returns HTTP 503 for 18 minutes after deployment changes connection-pool limit. Rollback restores service. Root cause includes missing load test and absent saturation alert. Actions: add load test, alert, deployment checklist, owners, and due dates.

14. RECOVERY AND METRICS
Service owner verifies health checks, error rates, latency, queue depth, and customer functionality. Track time to acknowledge, time to mitigate, recurrence, change-related incidents, action completion, and alert quality. Use metrics to improve systems, not rank individuals without context.
